The first release of Pulse: a menu bar app and a `pulse` command to watch temperatures and drive the fans of Apple silicon Macs, built on [smctl](https://github.com/leaperone/smctl).

## Highlights

- **Menu bar**: CPU, GPU and fan at a glance, with a heartbeat icon that warms up with the chip; a popover with temperatures, fans, the last minutes and the profile picker.
- **Profiles**: Auto (macOS), Silent, Balanced and Performance, plus your own curves (and `full` speed from the command line).
- **Curve editor**: shapes (linear, sigmoid, exponential, logarithmic, steps) or free points, in % or RPM, with a live preview, response settings (hysteresis, rise and fall rates, smoothing), undo and redo.
- **Stress test**: heat the Mac under a curve, watch it react, and compare with the previous run, in the app or with `pulse fan stress`.
- **Automation**: switch profiles when an app or a game is open, in Low Power Mode, on battery or on the adapter.
- **Safety**: a temperature guard that cannot be turned off hands the fans back to macOS and notifies you; quitting, killing or uninstalling never leaves the fans stuck.
- **Command line**: `pulse status`, `pulse fan curve …`, `pulse doctor` for bug reports, `--json` everywhere; works over SSH.
- **Updates**: Pulse tells you when a new version is out, and updates its fan control service by itself after an upgrade.

Tested on a Mac mini M4 and a MacBook Pro M1 Pro (macOS 27). MacBooks with an M3 or M4 use a fan unlock path that has not been tested on real hardware yet: `pulse doctor --test-fans` checks it, and its report is welcome in an issue.
